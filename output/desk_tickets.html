#101 · Bulldog tee customer_order

Requester
Tauhid Zaman
Ask
1 × CC-TEE-WHITE (Classic Bulldog Tee), size S — “Needs a tee in size S.”
Links
inventory/pricing (CC-TEE-WHITE, S) · invoice #501 (Bulldog Print Co, $840.00 rush reprint of the S tee, due 2026-08-28, open)
What the data says
Size S on hand = 0. The reprint that would refill it is on invoice 501, which is 3 days overdue vs desk.date_today 2026-08-31, so the vendor is holding it.

Expected

Boss calls first: Customer Service. It's a customer order with a real person waiting, so the person who owns the reply should own the ticket. Customer Service pulls the facts it needs instead of the Boss fanning out.

Delegations I expect:

  1. Boss → Customer Service: “Tauhid wants 1 tee in S, invoice 501 is linked.”
  2. Customer Service → Inventory: is S in stock, and what is invoice 501 holding? Inventory finds S = 0 and that 501 is the S reprint.
  3. Inventory → Accounting (the one real hand-off between specialists): the reprint is stuck behind an overdue $840 invoice. Accounting checks cash ($3,400, so it can be covered) and prepares the $840 payment for a human.
  4. Customer Service queues a reply: out of stock in S, the reprint is being released, ETA 2026-08-31 + 5 lead days = 2026-09-05, and M/L/XL are in stock if Tauhid wants one now.

Not called: Facilities — nothing about the shop space.

MCP tools I expect: get_ticket, get_desk_today, check_stock, get_invoice, list_vendors (lead days), get_cash_position, prepare_invoice_payment, queue_customer_message, list_pending_actions, record_ticket_outcome.

Human approval: pay invoice 501, $840.00 → checking $3,400.00 → $2,560.00.

Actual matches plan, +1 extra hop

Delegations that happened (4):

Reflection

1. How I'd evaluate the agents on each ticket

I graded each ticket on four things: correct outcome (right money prepared, right promise to the customer), rule-keeping (desk date, unpaid-invoice hold, human approval, cash floor, discount policy), honesty (every number traceable to an MCP tool result in the audit trail) and efficiency (model calls and hand-offs vs. what the ticket needed).

Overall, the agents never did anything wrong with money. Where they went sloppy (duplicate prepares, a $264 PO attempt with no cash), the MCP guardrails caught it. That's the lesson: prompts set intent, but code has to enforce the rules. The weakest part is cost. Ticket 103 used 2.4× the tokens of 102 for a decision a human manager makes in two minutes.

2. Actual vs. Expected, per ticket

  • 101: First call matched (Customer Service). The specialist hand-off I predicted, Inventory → Accounting about invoice 501, happened. One extra hop I didn't plan: Customer Service went straight to Accounting in parallel with Inventory. Tools matched except list_vendors: I assumed it was needed for the lead time, but get_invoice already joins the vendor's lead_days, so my plan was a step too pessimistic. Facilities stayed idle, as planned.
  • 102: First call (Facilities) and the Facilities → Accounting hand-off matched exactly, and the agents also skipped Customer Service, as I'd planned. Difference: Accounting → Facilities "confirm lease details" was a pointless loop-back that the prompt explicitly discourages. The tool set was identical to my plan.
  • 103: First call (Accounting), Accounting → Inventory, the refused purchase order, and Accounting → Customer Service all matched my plan, and the outcome was exactly what I wrote ($0 cash, override only). The difference was volume: Inventory came back to Accounting itself (planned as a reply, done as a new hand-off), and Customer Service re-checked with both Inventory and Accounting before writing. 6 hand-offs vs. my 3.

Pattern: the first call was right 3/3 (the Boss prompt's type → owner table works), and no ticket ever pulled in the agent I marked "not called". The drift is always extra verification, never wrong delegation.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools — and why

  • Ticket 102 (rent) is the clearest case. It's a linear checklist: get_lease → list_payments → get_cash_position → prepare_rent_payment. One "shop operator" agent with those four tools would do it in about 5 calls instead of 15. The Facilities/Accounting split added a hand-off and a loop-back but no judgment.
  • Ticket 101 is also mostly a single chain (stock → invoice → pay → reply). The split cost a duplicate prepare because two agents both thought they owned the invoice question. One agent couldn't have done that.
  • Ticket 103 is the only one where separate roles earned their keep: Accounting held the margin and cash view and Customer Service held the "don't leak internal numbers" voice, and keeping those apart made the club's message cleaner. Even there, a single agent with quote_bulk_price, check_stock, get_cash_position and queue_customer_message would have reached the same outcome, because the rules that mattered live in the tools.

Why: at Campus Customs' size (3 tickets, 1 account, 1 lease, 3 vendors) the data is small enough for one context window, and every hand-off re-sends context (that's where 66k tokens went). Multi-agent pays off when roles need different permissions (only Accounting can prepare money; that's real value) or truly parallel work. A cheaper design: one agent plus role-scoped tool permissions, or the Boss calling specialists only for decisions, not for re-checks.

4. Three new problems this team can solve with the tools I built

  1. "Do you have the Yale Cap for our 30-person welcome event, and can we get a deal?" Customer Service → Accounting: check_stock(CC-HAT-BLUE) shows 40 OS in Aisle C, so it can be filled today. quote_bulk_price(CC-HAT-BLUE, 30, 15) gives $18.70 with a 67.9% margin, inside policy, then request_price_override and a queued quote. Unlike 103, there's no reprint and no cash needed.
  2. A new Bulldog Print invoice arrives and the manager asks "can we pay it this week?" Accounting uses list_open_invoices + get_cash_position. After our run, checking is $160 with nothing committed until rent on 2026-10-02, so it can say exactly what's affordable and prepare it with prepare_invoice_payment once the invoice is on a ticket. It's the same flow as 101.
  3. A customer emails "I want a Crest Mug." Inventory finds CC-MUG-CREST OS = 0 and list_vendors finds Elm City Gifts (mugs, 3 lead days), so ETA = date_today + 3. prepare_purchase_order for a few mugs at $3.50 each fits inside the $160 free cash (e.g. 12 × $3.50 = $42), and Customer Service queues an honest reply with the date.

5. Three new problems this team cannot solve — and what it would need

  1. Collecting the AI Club's deposit so the 12-hoodie reprint can go ahead. Ticket 103 stalled because the only money tools are outflows. There's no table or tool for money coming in. It would need an orders + customer_payments table, a record_deposit tool (human-confirmed, like approvals), and a Payments/Billing agent (or Accounting with a Stripe-style payment-link tool). Then free cash becomes $160 + deposit, and prepare_purchase_order for $264 would pass.
  2. Actually shipping and tracking Tauhid's tee once the reprint lands. Nothing updates inventory.qty when goods arrive, and QuickShip CT (local courier) is in vendors with no way to book it. It would need receive_stock (increment qty against a PO), create_shipment / track_shipment tools for the courier, and a Fulfilment agent that watches POs and tells Customer Service when to send the "it shipped" message. Today the 101 reply can only say "around Sep 5".
  3. Disputing a late fee or renegotiating the lease with Elm City Properties. Facilities can read leases but has no lease terms (late fees, renewal dates), no document store, and no way to send email. It would need a contracts table or document retrieval (RAG over the PDF lease), a draft_landlord_email tool that goes through the same human-approval queue as customer messages, and probably a Legal/Contracts agent with read-only access to the lease document.
